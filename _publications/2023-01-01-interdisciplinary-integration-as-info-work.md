excerpt: 'In this article, I review the ways that interdisciplinary integration is studied as a collaborative task, as knowledge synthesis modeled by various conceptual frameworks, and as a series of outcomes measurable by citation networks or real-world indicators. I argue that each of these approaches is valuable but limited in their utility as a way of understanding and evaluating integration. I suggest borrowing from the field of information studies and adopting an alternate perspective of integration as a data- and informationmanagement practice as a means of addressing some of these limitations, and discuss the justification for and potential application of this approach.'
